      About this HEIC to JPG converter

      iPhones save photos as HEIC by default — great for storage, terrible for compatibility. Windows apps, older software and many websites still demand JPG. This free converter turns HEIC and HEIF photos into standard JPGs with adjustable quality, several files at once.

      Decoding runs on a HEIC decoder that executes entirely in your browser (the decoder file is served from this site, so the page works offline once loaded and makes zero external requests). That matters because the usual HEIC converters all require uploading your personal photos to their servers first — here they never leave your device.

      How to use

      1. Drop your .HEIC files into the upload box (or click to browse).
      2. Set the JPG quality (92 is a strong default; 80 for smaller files).
      3. Click Convert to JPG — the first conversion loads the decoder (~1MB), then each photo converts in seconds.
      4. Download each JPG from the results list.

      Limitations

      • The decoder (~1.3MB) loads on first use, so the first conversion takes a few seconds; later ones are fast.
      • Live Photos and burst sequences convert the primary still image only.
      • HEIC metadata (location, camera settings) is generally not carried into the JPG.
      • Very old browsers without WebAssembly support cannot run the decoder.

      Frequently asked questions

      How do I convert HEIC to JPG on iPhone or Windows?

      Drop the .HEIC files into the tool above and click Convert to JPG. It works in any modern browser on iPhone, Android, Windows or Mac — no app to install, no upload, no signup.

      Why does my iPhone save photos as HEIC?

      Apple switched the default camera format to HEIC (High Efficiency Image Container) because it stores the same quality in roughly half the file size of JPG. The tradeoff is compatibility: many Windows programs and websites still expect JPG.

      Will converting HEIC to JPG lose quality?

      JPG is a lossy format, so there is a small, usually invisible loss at quality 92. If you need pixel-perfect archival copies, keep the original HEIC files too.

      Are my photos uploaded anywhere?

      No. Decoding happens 100% in your browser with a locally-served decoder. Your photos never leave your device.
