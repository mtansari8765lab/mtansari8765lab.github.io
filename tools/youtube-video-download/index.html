YouTube Video Download Guide

This page does not download videos. It explains the official, lawful ways to get a copy of a YouTube video — and is honest about what you cannot do. YouTube lets you download videos you uploaded. You cannot download other people's videos as files, and this site will not try to do it for you.

Check your YouTube link

Paste a YouTube link and we will check it is a real video link, tidy it into its canonical form, and point you at the right official page for that video. Nothing is sent to YouTube — the check runs entirely in your browser, so no title or thumbnail is fetched and nothing about your link leaves your device.

Download your own YouTube video

If you uploaded the video, YouTube gives you a download button of your own. It is the only official way to get an MP4 file of a video, and it works for exactly one kind of video: yours.

  1. Sign in to YouTube Studio with the Google account that owns the channel.
  2. From the left panel, select Content.
  3. Find the video, open its menu (the three dots), and select Download.
  4. Save the MP4 file. You get 720p or 360p, depending on the size of the original upload.

You can download each video up to five times in 24 hours. After the fifth download, the option disappears until a day has passed. Full details are in YouTube's own help page, Download YouTube videos that you've uploaded.

Why your own video might not download

YouTube blocks the download in four cases:

  • The video has been removed from YouTube.
  • The video has a copyright or Community Guidelines strike against it.
  • The video uses a pre-approved audio track.
  • You have already downloaded that video five times in the last 24 hours.

If you blurred or trimmed a video in YouTube's editor, you can download the edited version and re-upload it to publish those changes.

Download all your uploads at once with Google Takeout

Downloading one video at a time is slow if you run a channel with dozens of uploads. Google Takeout exports all of them in one archive:

  1. Go to Google Takeout and sign in to the Google account that owns the channel.
  2. Select YouTube and choose the data you want — your uploaded videos are included.
  3. Choose a delivery method (a download link by email is the simplest) and create the export.
  4. Google emails you when the archive is ready. Depending on the size of your channel, that can take minutes or days.

Takeout is also the right tool if you want a backup of your whole channel before deleting videos or closing an account. Google's guide is How to download your Google data.

Watch videos offline with YouTube Premium

For videos you did not upload, there is no official way to save an MP4 file. What YouTube does offer is offline viewing inside its own app, through a YouTube Premium membership:

  1. Sign in to your Premium account in the YouTube app.
  2. Open the video's watch page and tap Download.
  3. Find it later under the Library / You tab. On a computer, the same Download button appears in Chrome, Edge, Firefox and Opera.

Downloaded videos play offline for up to 29 days — 48 hours in some regions — before the app needs to reconnect and re-check them, and a video that is no longer available for offline playback is removed on the next sync. Whether the Download button appears at all depends on your region and on the individual video; some videos are not eligible. YouTube's help page is Watch videos offline with YouTube Premium.

What this guide will not do

Searching "download YouTube video" returns dozens of sites promising an MP4 or MP3 of any video. They work by pulling the video stream out of YouTube without permission, which breaks YouTube's Terms of Service — and many of them are unsafe in practice, wrapping the download in pop-up ads, fake download buttons or malware. This page deliberately offers none of that: no stream extraction, no third-party downloader, no file for a video you do not own. If you need a copy of someone else's video, ask the creator — they can send you the file or grant permission, and that is the only route that respects their work and keeps your device safe.

Copyright in one paragraph

Every video on YouTube belongs to someone — usually the person who uploaded it. Downloading your own uploads is your right. Downloading, re-uploading or reusing someone else's video without their permission infringes their copyright, even if you credit them, even if you do not make money from it, and even if the video is freely watchable. Offline viewing through YouTube Premium is a licence to watch, not a licence to copy or redistribute.

Frequently asked questions

Can I download a YouTube video I did not upload?

Not as a file. YouTube lets you download only videos you uploaded yourself, through YouTube Studio or Google Takeout. Other people's videos can be saved for offline viewing inside the YouTube app with a YouTube Premium membership, where the video is available — but that is not an MP4 file you can keep or share.

How do I download my own YouTube video?

Sign in to YouTube Studio, open Content, open the menu next to the video and choose Download. You get an MP4 file in 720p or 360p, depending on the video size. You can download each video up to five times in 24 hours.

How do I download all my YouTube videos at once?

Use Google Takeout at takeout.google.com: sign in, select YouTube, choose the data you want, and create an export. Google emails you a link to download the archive, which includes the videos you uploaded. Large channels can take hours or days to prepare.

Can I watch YouTube videos offline without downloading them as files?

Yes, with YouTube Premium. Open the video's watch page in the YouTube app (or in Chrome, Edge, Firefox or Opera on a computer) and tap Download. The video plays offline inside the app for up to 29 days, or 48 hours in some regions, before the app needs to reconnect and re-check it. Availability depends on your region and on the individual video.

Why can't I download my own video?

YouTube blocks the download when the video has been removed, when it has a copyright or Community Guidelines strike, when it uses a pre-approved audio track, or when you have already downloaded that video five times in the last 24 hours. Wait a day after the fifth download and the option returns.

Are third-party YouTube downloader sites safe or legal?

No — not reliably on either count. Downloading videos you do not own with third-party tools breaks YouTube's Terms of Service, and many downloader sites bundle ads, fake download buttons or malware. If you need a copy of a video you did not make, ask the creator for permission or for the file directly.
