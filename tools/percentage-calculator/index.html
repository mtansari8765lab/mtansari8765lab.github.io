About this percentage calculator

Percentages show up everywhere: tips, discounts, tax, interest rates, grade curves, and month-over-month growth. The three calculators above cover the three questions people actually ask. "What is 19% of 199.99?" is the tip-or-tax question. "45 is what percent of 180?" is the progress question — how far through a goal you are. "100 to 125" is the change question — how much something grew or shrank.

One trap to watch: percent change is not the same as percentage points. If a rate moves from 50% to 60%, that is a 10 percentage-point move but a 20% relative increase. Mixing the two up is one of the most common mistakes in business reporting, and the third calculator above uses the relative definition.

How to use

  1. Pick the question you want answered: X% of Y, X as a percent of Y, or percent change.
  2. Enter the numbers — decimals are fine (19% of 199.99 works).
  3. Press Calculate. Results are rounded to a sensible precision.

Limitations

  • Percent change from an original value of zero is undefined — the calculator will tell you.
  • "X is what percent of Y" needs a non-zero Y (division by zero is rejected).
  • Results are rounded to 4 decimal places; for money, use the discount calculator which rounds to cents.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate X percent of a number?

Multiply the number by X and divide by 100. For example, 19% of 199.99 is 199.99 × 19 ÷ 100 = 37.9981. The first box on this page does it for you.

How do I find what percent one number is of another?

Divide the part by the whole and multiply by 100. For example, 45 is (45 ÷ 180) × 100 = 25% of 180.

What is the difference between percent change and percentage points?

Percent change is relative: going from 50% to 60% is a 20% increase. Percentage points are the simple difference: that same move is a 10 percentage-point increase. They answer different questions.

How do I calculate a percent increase or decrease?

Subtract the original value from the new value, divide by the original value, and multiply by 100. A positive answer is an increase, a negative one a decrease.

Are my numbers uploaded anywhere?

No. The calculator runs 100% in your browser with JavaScript. Your numbers never leave your device, are never stored, and never sent to any server.
