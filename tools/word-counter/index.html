About this word counter

Word limits are everywhere: essays capped at 1,000 words, abstracts limited to 250, social posts trimmed to fit, and resumes kept to one page. Counting by hand is slow and error-prone, and opening a full word processor just to check a paragraph is overkill. This free online word counter solves that — paste or type your text and every stat updates live: total words, characters with and without spaces, sentences, paragraphs, plus estimated reading and speaking time.

The counter is Unicode-safe, so emoji and non-Latin scripts like Chinese, Arabic or Hindi are counted by actual characters rather than broken byte fragments. Reading time is estimated at 200 words per minute (average adult reading speed) and speaking time at 130 words per minute (a comfortable presentation pace) — genuinely useful when you are timing a speech, a video script, or a podcast outline and need to know whether your draft fits the slot.

Because the whole tool runs in your browser, it also works offline once the page has loaded, and your text is never uploaded anywhere — safe for drafts, client work, and anything confidential.

How to use

  1. Type directly into the box, or paste text from any document, email or web page.
  2. Watch the stats update live as you type — no button needed.
  3. Use Copy stats to copy a summary (e.g. "Words: 248, Characters: 1,532") to your clipboard.
  4. Use Clear to empty the box and start over.

Limitations

  • A "word" is any sequence of non-whitespace characters, so hyphenated compounds like "well-known" count as one word.
  • Sentence detection splits on . ! ? and … — abbreviations such as "Mr." or "e.g." may create extra sentence breaks.
  • Paragraphs are blocks separated by blank lines; single line breaks do not start a new paragraph.
  • Reading and speaking times are estimates based on average speeds; dense or technical text takes longer.

Frequently asked questions

How does the QuickTools word counter count words?

A word is any sequence of non-whitespace characters. So "hello-world" counts as one word, while "hello world" counts as two. This matches how most word processors count.

Is the word counter accurate for essays and assignments?

Yes for the standard definition of a word. Very long hyphenated compounds may count differently than in Microsoft Word or Google Docs, which use their own proprietary rules — if your institution is strict, do a final check in the required program.

How is reading time calculated?

Reading time assumes an average adult reading speed of 200 words per minute; speaking time assumes 130 words per minute, a typical presentation pace. Both are estimates — dense or technical text takes longer.

Is my text uploaded anywhere?

No. The counter runs 100% in your browser with JavaScript. Your text never leaves your device, is never stored, and never sent to any server.
