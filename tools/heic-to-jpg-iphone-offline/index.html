      Convert iPhone HEIC photos to JPG without uploading them

      Your iPhone saves photos as HEIC by default. The format keeps files about half the size of JPG, which is why Apple chose it — but plenty of places still will not accept it: upload forms, older apps, email attachments that arrive as broken icons, and Windows computers that need a separate codec just to open the file. Converting to JPG fixes the compatibility problem, and doing it in your browser means the photos never have to travel to somebody else's server first.

      That distinction matters. Most free HEIC converters are upload services: your photos go to their server, get converted there, and you download the result. This tool works the other way round. The converter program itself — a 1.3 MB decoder file — is served from this site and runs inside your browser. Your photos are read from your phone, decoded on your phone, and the JPGs are offered straight back as downloads. We verified this by tracing the tool's code and watching its network traffic during a real conversion: no photo data is sent anywhere.

      How to pick your photos on iPhone

      Open this page in Safari on your iPhone and tap the upload box. The standard file picker opens, and you can choose photos from your photo library or from the Files app. Photos stored in iCloud are downloaded to your phone when you pick them, and the conversion still happens on the device — iCloud hands the file to your browser, not to us. You can select several photos at once; each one converts to its own JPG.

      1. Tap the upload box and choose your HEIC photos.
      2. Set the JPG quality — 92 is the default and suits most uses.
      3. Tap Convert to JPG.
      4. Download each JPG from the results list.

      What the offline part really means

      Once this page and its decoder have loaded, conversion keeps working even with the network switched off — we tested exactly that in desktop Chrome, converting a real HEIC file with the network cut and getting a valid JPG back. The same page code runs in Safari on your iPhone. Two honest limits: the first load still needs the internet, because the page and decoder have to download first; and the offline test itself was done on a desktop browser, not on a phone, so on iPhone we promise no upload, not a lab-tested offline run.

      What changes when a photo becomes a JPG

      The picture itself comes across at the quality you choose, and the filename stays the same apart from the .jpg ending. Three things do not carry over. The photo's metadata — date taken, location and camera settings — is dropped, so keep the original HEIC if that record matters. Live Photos convert to their main still image only. And very saturated colours can shift slightly, because HEIC can store wide-gamut colour and the JPG is written in standard sRGB.

      If a photo will not convert

      • A file that is not really a HEIC file is skipped when you add it — only .heic and .heif files are accepted.
      • A damaged or half-downloaded file fails with an error, and the rest of the batch still converts.
      • Very old browsers without WebAssembly support cannot run the decoder — update the browser and try again.

      Frequently asked questions

      Do my iPhone photos get uploaded when I convert them here?

      No. The decoder runs inside your browser on your phone, and we verified this by tracing the tool's code and watching its network traffic during a real conversion: no photo data is sent to any server. The only files that travel are the page itself and the decoder, both downloaded from this site.

      Can I convert HEIC to JPG on my iPhone without the internet?

      Partly. Once the page and its 1.3 MB decoder have loaded, conversion keeps working with the network switched off — we tested exactly that in desktop Chrome and got a valid JPG back. The first load still needs the internet, and the offline test was run on a desktop browser, not on a physical iPhone. What is guaranteed on iPhone is that nothing is uploaded.

      How do I select HEIC photos stored in iCloud?

      Tap the upload box, then choose Browse in the Files app and open iCloud Drive, or pick from your photo library. iCloud downloads the file to your phone when you pick it, and the conversion still happens on the device — the file goes to your browser, not to our server.

      Will converting lose quality or delete my originals?

      There is a small, usually invisible quality loss at the default quality of 92, because JPG is a lossy format. Your originals are never touched — the tool only reads them, and the HEIC files stay exactly where they were. Keep the originals if the photo's date, location or camera data matters, because that metadata is not carried into the JPG.
