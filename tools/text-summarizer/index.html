How this summarizer works (honestly)

This is an extractive, frequency-based summarizer — a classic text-processing technique, not artificial intelligence and not machine learning. It works in four transparent steps:

  1. Split your text into sentences.
  2. Count how often each significant word appears in the whole document (common filler words like "the", "and", "is" are ignored via a stopword list).
  3. Score every sentence by the frequencies of the words it contains, normalized for length so long sentences don't automatically win.
  4. Keep the top-scoring sentences and return them in their original order, so the summary reads like a condensed version of your text.

The upside of this old-fashioned approach: the summary is always a faithful subset of your original sentences. It never invents facts, never hallucinates, and you can trace every summary sentence back to its source. Use Sentences mode when you want an exact length ("give me 3 sentences"), and Percent mode when the document length varies — 25% of a 40-sentence report keeps 10 sentences automatically.

What extractive summarization can't do

  • It doesn't understand meaning. It scores words by frequency, so a sentence can rank highly just for repeating terms — and a crucial sentence stated once, subtly, can be missed.
  • It can't rephrase. Unlike an AI model, it never rewrites or combines ideas; the summary is stitched from original sentences, which can feel choppy.
  • It struggles with short text. Below about five sentences there is little worth compressing — frequency statistics need volume to be meaningful.
  • It misses implied points. Dialogue, humor, poetry, and arguments where the conclusion is implied rather than stated will summarize poorly.
  • Abbreviations can split sentences. "Mr.", "e.g." and similar may create extra sentence breaks, slightly skewing the ranking.

For study notes, meeting minutes, and news articles it is genuinely useful. For nuanced or creative writing, read the original.

Frequently asked questions

How does this text summarizer work?

It is an extractive, frequency-based summarizer. It splits your text into sentences, counts how often each significant word appears in the whole document, scores every sentence by the frequencies of the words it contains, and keeps the top-scoring sentences in their original order. No machine learning and no AI model are involved.

Is this an AI summarizer?

No. This tool uses classic extractive summarization: word-frequency scoring with stopword removal and length normalization. It cannot understand meaning the way a large language model does — which is exactly why its output is always a faithful subset of your original sentences and never invents facts.

What is the difference between sentences mode and percent mode?

Sentences mode keeps an exact number of sentences. Percent mode keeps that share of the total — 25% of a 20-sentence article keeps 5 sentences — and adapts automatically to document length.

Why does the summary keep sentences in their original order?

Reading order matters for comprehension. The tool ranks sentences by importance to choose which survive, then restores document order so the summary reads like a condensed version of your text.

What text works best with extractive summarization?

Factual, information-dense writing: articles, reports, meeting notes, research summaries. It works poorly on very short texts, dialogue, poetry, and texts where the key point is implied rather than stated.

Is my text uploaded anywhere?

No. The summarizer runs 100% in your browser with JavaScript. Your text never leaves your device, is never stored, and never sent to any server.
